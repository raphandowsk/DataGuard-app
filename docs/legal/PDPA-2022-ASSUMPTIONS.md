**Framework:** Tanzania Personal Data Protection Act (`TZ-PDPA`), version `2022`

> This control matrix is a product implementation mapping of the Tanzania Personal Data Protection Act, 2022. It is intended to support compliance management and does not constitute legal advice, legal certification, or an authoritative interpretation of Tanzanian law.

## 2. Standing rules that constrain this matrix

These are hard constraints on DataGuard, not preferences. Each exists because the Act is silent and the temptation to fill the gap from another regime is strong.

1. **No invented retention periods.** The Act prescribes none. Section 28(1) points to *the relevant laws* or *the regulations*; section 28(2) and section 64(2)(j) leave retention and disposal to the Minister. DataGuard must never pre-populate a number of years for any record type.
2. **No invented adequacy decisions.** The Act contains no list of countries with adequate data protection. Adequacy is assessed by the data controller against the six factors in section 32(2), subject to the Commission's powers under sections 31(1) and 32(5) and to regulations under section 32(3). DataGuard must never hard-code a country as adequate or inadequate.
3. **No imported breach-notification deadline.** Section 27(5) says *without any undue delay*. There is no 72-hour rule in this Act, and no duty to notify affected data subjects. Neither may be introduced by analogy to another regime.
4. **No imported consent doctrine.** Section 30(1) requires *prior written consent* for sensitive personal data. The Act does not define freely given, specific, informed or unambiguous consent, and does not import those tests. Section 30(5) provides six express exceptions; sensitive processing is not automatically unlawful without consent.
5. **Recommended evidence is not a legal requirement.** Only controls whose `evidence_required` is `REQUIRED_BY_ACT` reflect a record the Act itself calls for. Everything else is a DataGuard suggestion.
6. **DataGuard risk levels are not statutory classifications.** `default_risk_level` is a product recommendation. The Act attaches no risk grading to any provision.
7. **Penalties are stated only where the Act attaches them to that provision.** Section 47 sets a ceiling of TZS 100,000,000 on administrative penalty notices under section 46. It does not attach that figure to any individual control. Sections 43, 60, 61 and 63 carry their own express criminal penalties and are cited only on the controls they actually govern.
8. **No framework cross-contamination.** GDPR, ISO 27001, NIST and CCPA concepts must not appear as requirements. Where a familiar concept has no basis in this Act, it is either absent or explicitly marked `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` in section 6 below.

## 4. Open questions

Each is recorded inline in the `notes` field of the control named. None has been resolved by assumption.

### Registration and governance

- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-020-001`, s.20)** — The Act states that an aggrieved person *may appeal in writing to the Minister* but sets no time limit for doing so. Any internal deadline an organisation adopts is its own commitment.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-021-001`, s.21)** — *Public institution* is not defined for the purposes of the deemed-registration provision, and the Act does not say what particulars a deemed-registered institution must supply for the register under section 15.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-027-011`, s.27(3))** — The Act requires a data protection officer but sets no qualifications, no independence requirement, and does not say whether the role may be shared across a group of companies or outsourced.

### Security and breach

- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-027-016`, s.27(5))** — Four gaps in one subsection: *security breach* is not defined; *without any undue delay* is not quantified; the content and form of the notification are not prescribed; and there is no duty to notify affected data subjects. Do not import any of these from another regime.

### Correction and data integrity

- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-029-001`, s.29(1))** — No time limit is set for deciding a correction application, and the provision is framed as a discretion (*may amend*) exercisable once the controller *is satisfied*. The threshold for being satisfied is not stated.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-029-002`, s.29(2))** — The Act forbids permanent deletion of the pre-amendment text but does not say for how long the prior version must be kept, nor how that interacts with the disposal duty under section 28 or with a preservation order under section 59. **This is the most architecturally significant open question in the matrix** — see section 5.

### Sensitive personal data

- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-030-002`, s.30(1))** — *Prior written consent* is required but the Act does not define the form or content of written consent, nor whether electronic capture qualifies.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-030-004`, s.30(4))** — The Act does not prescribe how age or capacity is to be verified before accepting representative consent. *Child* takes its meaning from the Child Act, which is outside this Act.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-030-006`, s.30(5)(e))** — On the wording of the Act the scientific research exception is conditional on the Commission having issued *special guidelines*. Whether such guidelines exist is a question of fact outside the Act. **DataGuard must not presume the exception is available.**

### Transborder data flow

- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-031-004`, s.31(3))** — *Provisional evaluation* of necessity is required of the controller, but the Act does not say what it must contain or when it ceases to be provisional.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-032-005`, s.32(5))** — The safeguards route runs through Commission authorisation, but the Act prescribes no application procedure, no form of safeguards, no decision period, and publishes no approved standard contractual clauses.

### Data subject rights

- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-033-001`, s.33)** — No response time, no prescribed form of request, and no fee regime for access requests.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-033-003`, s.33(1)(c))** — *Significantly affecting* is not defined, and the required depth of the *logic involved* description is not stated.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-034-001`, s.34)** — *Substantial damage* is not defined. Section 34(2) disapplies the right in *the exceptions provided under this Act* without identifying which exceptions it means.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-035-001`, s.35(1))** — No period is set within which direct marketing must cease after a request.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-035-002`, s.35(2))** — The Act permits agreements for the use of personal data for pecuniary benefit but sets no conditions, limits or safeguards for them.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-036-002`, s.36(2)(a))** — *As soon as practicable* is not defined.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-036-003`, s.36(2)(b))** — The Act does not say what reconsideration must involve or within what period it must be completed.

### Investigation and enforcement

- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-042-002`, s.42(3))** — The provision states that *no personal data shall be withheld from the Commission*, *notwithstanding any other written law*. The Act does not address how this interacts with legal professional privilege. Escalate to counsel rather than deciding in the moment.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-048-001`, s.48)** — No time limit for applying for a review of a Commission decision.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-049-001`, s.49)** — No period stated within which an appeal to the High Court must be lodged.

### Exemptions and codes

- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-058-001`, s.58)** — Section 58(1) opens *Nothing under this section shall exempt...*, which sits awkwardly with the exemptions granted by section 58(2). DataGuard adopts the safer reading — recorded at `PDPA-058-002` — that the principles and the security duties continue to apply to exempt processing. A narrower reading is arguable; take advice before relying on one.
- **`OPEN_QUESTION` (`PDPA-065-004`, s.65(2))** — No deadline for submitting the code of ethics to the Commission, no prescribed form of submission, and no stated consequence if the Commission does not respond.

## 5. Section 29(2) — architectural consequence for DataGuard

Section 29(2) is the only provision in the Act that dictates system behaviour directly:

> The data controller shall, when making an amendment to personal data in a document under this section, ensure that he does not permanently delete the record of the text of the document as it existed prior to the amendment.

**Statutory requirement (`ACT_EXPLICIT`, `PDPA-029-002`):** the pre-amendment text must not be permanently deleted.

**DataGuard implementation guidance (not a statutory prescription):** maintain an auditable history or version of corrected information rather than permanently overwriting the previous record. In practice this means append-only history tables, versioned documents, or an equivalent immutable audit trail on any system holding amendable personal data.

**Consequence for the DataGuard product itself:** compliance records that are corrected must retain an auditable history rather than being overwritten. This applies to assessment responses, evidence metadata and remediation records, because those may themselves contain personal data.

**Unresolved tension.** Three duties pull in different directions and the Act does not sequence them:

| Duty | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| s.28 + s.64(2)(j) — retention and disposal | Delete at end of period |
| s.29(2) — preservation of prior version | Do not permanently delete |
| s.59 — court preservation order | Do not delete for up to 90 days, extendable |

DataGuard's position, recorded at `PDPA-028-004` and `PDPA-059-001`: **a preservation order overrides scheduled disposal, and the section 29(2) prior version is treated as part of the record for retention purposes.** This is `INTERPRETATION` and should be confirmed with counsel for any organisation with a material archive.

## 6. Concepts deliberately NOT included

Each of the following is familiar from other privacy regimes and has **no basis in this Act**. They are recorded here so that their absence reads as a decision rather than an oversight, and so they are not quietly reintroduced later.

| Concept | Status | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data protection impact assessment (DPIA) | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | No provision requires one. Section 27(2)(b) requires potential risks to the data subject to be taken into account when setting security measures — that is captured at `PDPA-027-010` and is narrower than a DPIA regime. |
| Records of processing activities as a statutory duty | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | A processing register is DataGuard-recommended evidence throughout. The Act requires the *Commission* to maintain a register of controllers and processors (s.15); it does not require the controller to maintain a register of its processing. |
| Notification of a breach to affected data subjects | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | Section 27(5) requires notification to the Commission only. |
| A 72-hour breach notification deadline | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | The Act says *without any undue delay* and fixes no period. |
| Right to data portability | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | Part VI does not include it. |
| Right to erasure exercisable directly against the controller | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | Under sections 37(3) and 38(1) rectification, blocking, erasure and destruction are ordered by the **Commission** on the application of a data subject. There is no free-standing right to demand erasure from the controller. Section 29 provides a correction route, not an erasure route. |
| Privacy by design and by default | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | Not stated as a duty. Section 27(2) requires certain factors to be taken into account when setting security measures. |
| Mandatory data protection training | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | Training records appear as recommended evidence. No provision requires training. |
| Statutory response deadlines for data subject rights | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | The Act sets none. The only express periods are the Commission's 90 days for a complaint (s.39(3)-(4)), the minimum 21 days in an enforcement notice (s.45(2)(c)), the 10 working days for return of documents (s.42(4)), and the 90-day preservation order (s.59(2)). |
| Joint controller regime | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | Section 3 contemplates a controller acting *alone or jointly with others* but the Act allocates no obligations between joint controllers. |
| Sub-processor authorisation regime | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | Section 27(4) governs the controller-processor contract. It says nothing about sub-processors. Controlling them contractually is recommended at `PDPA-027-014` as guidance. |
| Certification or seals | `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` | The Act provides for codes of ethics under section 65, not for certification schemes. |

## 7. Interpretation decisions taken

Three controls are classified `INTERPRETATION`. Each is listed with the reading adopted, so it can be challenged.

| Control | Provision | Reading adopted | Why an interpretation was needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `PDPA-015-002` | s.15(2), s.17 | Registered particulars should be reviewed on the assumption that they are publicly inspectable. | Section 17 confers a power on the Commission to permit inspection; it imposes no duty on the organisation. The control exists to prompt awareness and is marked `required: false`. |
| `PDPA-018-001` | s.18 | The organisation should hold a pre-agreed response to deregistration, because deregistration reinstates the section 14(1) prohibition on processing. | Section 18 confers a power on the Commission and imposes no duty. The consequence for the organisation is derived from section 14(1). |
| `PDPA-030-010` | s.30 with s.27(3) | A consolidated sensitive-processing record is the practical means of demonstrating compliance with section 30. | The Act nowhere requires a sensitive data register. Without one, an organisation cannot answer the Commission activity by activity. |

Two further readings are recorded in control notes rather than as separate `INTERPRETATION` controls:

- **`PDPA-038-003` (s.38(4))** — section 38(4) is drafted in mandatory terms (*shall be required to notify*) and does not on its face require a further Commission order, in contrast to section 37(4) which is expressly conditional on the Commission so ordering. DataGuard adopts the mandatory reading. A narrower reading is arguable.
- **`PDPA-060-004` (s.60(4))** — section 60(4) refers to data obtained *in breach of subsection (1)*, but subsection (1) addresses disclosure by a data controller rather than obtaining. The scope of the cross-reference is not elaborated in the Act.

## 8. Items that should NOT be automated

These require human legal judgement. DataGuard should surface them for review and must not compute a compliance verdict on them.

| Area | Control(s) | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Union vs non-union matters in Zanzibar | `PDPA-002-001` | A constitutional question, not a data protection one. |
| Whether a destination country's legal framework is adequate | `PDPA-031-002`, `PDPA-032-002` | Requires assessment of foreign law under six statutory factors. Never pre-populate. |
| Whether processing falls within a section 58 exemption | `PDPA-058-001` | The national security, public interest and crime-prevention limbs are legally loaded. |
| Whether a section 30(5) exception applies | `PDPA-030-005`, `PDPA-030-006`, `PDPA-030-007` | Requires assessment of necessity and, for research, of Commission guidelines that may not exist. |
| Whether a section 23(3) exception to direct collection applies | `PDPA-023-003` | *Not reasonably practicable* and *would prejudice the lawful purpose* are open-textured. |
| Whether a decision *significantly affects* a data subject | `PDPA-033-003`, `PDPA-036-001` | Undefined in the Act. |
| Whether processing is likely to cause *substantial damage* | `PDPA-034-001` | Undefined in the Act. |
| The interaction of s.42(3) with legal professional privilege | `PDPA-042-002` | Unresolved by the Act. |
| Retention periods drawn from sectoral laws | `PDPA-028-001`, `PDPA-028-006` | Those laws are outside this Act. Never pre-populate a period. |
| Any assertion that the organisation *is compliant* | all | Completing the controls evidences a state of implementation. It is not a legal determination of compliance, and DataGuard must not present it as one. |

## 9. Assumptions about the source text

1. **Version.** The English text of GN. No. 395B (13 June 2023) has been used. It is a translation published under section 84(4) of the Interpretation of Laws Act, Cap. 1.
2. **Amendments.** No amendment to Act No. 11 of 2022 has been reflected. Whether any amendment or subsidiary legislation has been made since the source text is a question of fact that must be confirmed before relying on this matrix. This is tracked by `PDPA-064-001`.
3. **Section numbering.** Section 45 is headed *Notice of enforcement* in the arrangement of sections and *Enforcement notice* in the body. The matrix uses the body heading and cites section 45 in both cases.
4. **Schedule.** The Schedule (made under section 8(6)) concerns proceedings of the Board and imposes no duty on data controllers or data processors. It is recorded as reviewed and not mapped.
5. **Typographical fidelity.** Where the source text contains apparent drafting infelicities (for example *for the purpose of prevent or detect crimes* in section 58(2)(d)), the substance has been mapped without correction of the wording.

## 10. Review triggers

A new matrix version must be issued when any of the following occurs:

- Regulations are made under section 64, or under any of sections 14(3), 15(2), 16(2), 18, 28(2), 29(1), 30(3), 32(3), 34(1), 35(1), 36(1), 40 or 58(3).
- The Commission issues special guidelines under section 30(5)(e).
- The Commission publishes any determination under section 31(1) or authorisation practice under section 32(5).
- The Act is amended.
- A court or the Commission resolves any `OPEN_QUESTION` in section 4 above.
- Tanzanian counsel advises that an `INTERPRETATION` in section 7 above is wrong.

Record every such change in [PDPA-2022-CHANGELOG.md](PDPA-2022-CHANGELOG.md). Control IDs are permanent: supersede a control and issue a new framework version rather than repurposing an existing ID.
