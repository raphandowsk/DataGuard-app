# Tanzania PDPA 2022 Control Matrix - Changelog

**Framework:** Tanzania Personal Data Protection Act (`TZ-PDPA`)
**Framework version:** `2022` (the Act itself)
**Matrix versions:** tracked below

> This control matrix is a product implementation mapping of the Tanzania Personal Data Protection Act, 2022. It is intended to support compliance management and does not constitute legal advice, legal certification, or an authoritative interpretation of Tanzanian law.

This file is maintained by hand. It is **not** regenerated by `scripts/generate-pdpa-artifacts.mjs`.

---

## Versioning rules

These rules exist so that historical assessments stay interpretable. Breaking them silently invalidates every assessment taken against an earlier version.

1. **Framework version tracks the law.** `framework_version` is `2022` and changes only if the Act itself is amended or replaced. A new Act, or an amending Act, produces a new framework version — not a new matrix version.
2. **Matrix version tracks this mapping.** `matrix_version` follows semantic versioning:
   - **MAJOR** — a control is retired, its legal meaning changes, or the schema changes in a way that breaks existing responses.
   - **MINOR** — controls are added, or an existing control's guidance, evidence, risk or task changes without altering the legal requirement it maps to.
   - **PATCH** — corrections to wording that do not change meaning.
3. **Control IDs are permanent.** A control ID is never reused for a different legal requirement, and is never changed for cosmetic reasons. If a control becomes wrong, set `control_status` to `RETIRED` and issue a new ID.
4. **Historical controls are never deleted.** Retired controls stay in the matrix with `control_status: RETIRED` so that past assessment responses continue to resolve.
5. **Every entry states its legal trigger.** An entry must say what changed in the law, the regulations or the interpretation, not merely what changed in the file.
6. **Regeneration is not a change.** Re-running the generator without editing `scripts/pdpa-2022.source.mjs` produces identical output and requires no entry.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-09-03

Initial control matrix. Derived solely from the Personal Data Protection Act, Chapter 44 (Act No. 11 of 2022), English version published by GN. No. 395B of 13 June 2023, in force 1 May 2023 (GN. No. 326 of 2023).

### Added

**123 controls across 43 sections and 96 statutory requirements.**

| Part | Sections mapped | Controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I - Preliminary Provisions | 2, 5 | 9 |
| III - Registration of Data Controllers and Data Processors | 14-16, 18-21 | 11 |
| IV - Collection, Use, Disclosure and Retention of Personal Data | 22-30 | 47 |
| V - Transborder Data Flow | 31-32 | 12 |
| VI - Rights of Data Subjects | 33-38 | 17 |
| VII - Investigation of Complaints | 39, 40, 42, 43, 45-50 | 12 |
| IX - Miscellaneous Provisions | 58-62, 64, 65 | 15 |

**Source classification:** `ACT_EXPLICIT` 105 · `ACT_DERIVED` 14 · `INTERPRETATION` 3 · `IMPLEMENTATION_GUIDANCE` 1.

**Applicability:** `UNIVERSAL` 81 · `CONDITIONAL` 39 · `ROLE_SPECIFIC` 3.

**Areas given detailed decomposition, as scoped for this release:**

- **Section 27 (security)** — 17 controls. Each harm named in section 27(1) is separated (`PDPA-027-002` negligent loss, `-003` unauthorised destruction, `-004` alteration, `-005` access, `-006` processing), each section 27(2) factor is assessed separately (`-007` technology, `-008` cost, `-009` nature of data, `-010` risk to data subject), and the DPO, processor-contract and breach-notification duties are separated from one another (`-011` to `-017`).
- **Section 30 (sensitive personal data)** — 10 controls covering identification, prior written consent, withdrawal, representative consent, the six section 30(5) exceptions, the research and medical limbs individually, absolute prohibitions under section 30(3), enhanced security, and documentation.
- **Sections 31-32 (transborder flow)** — 12 controls covering transfer identification, destination assessment, the statutory grounds, the controller's provisional evaluation, recipient obligations, the six-factor adequacy test, the derogations, and Commission authorisation.
- **Part VI (data subject rights)** — 17 controls, with access, prevention of processing, direct marketing, automated decision making, compensation, rectification, blocking, erasure and destruction each mapped separately.
- **Section 65 (code of ethics)** — 5 controls covering existence, scope, approval, submission to the Commission, and versioning through the Commission's consideration process.

### Deliberate omissions

- Sections 1, 3, 4, 6-13, 17, 41, 44, 47, 51-57, 63 and the Schedule create no assessable obligation on a data controller or data processor. Each is recorded with its reason in the coverage audit and in the `UNMAPPED PROVISIONS` section of the traceability document.
- Section 47 is carried as a reference record (`PDPA-047-001`, `control_status: REFERENCE`) so the statutory ceiling on administrative fines is available for board reporting without being scored as a control.
- No concept from GDPR, ISO 27001, NIST or CCPA has been introduced. Twelve such concepts are recorded as `NOT_EXPLICITLY_SUPPORTED_BY_ACT` in [PDPA-2022-ASSUMPTIONS.md](PDPA-2022-ASSUMPTIONS.md) section 6.

### Known gaps carried forward

- 21 controls carry `REGULATORY_DETAIL_PENDING`. Their duty is established by the Act; their operational parameters await regulations under section 64 and related provisions.
- 22 controls carry at least one `OPEN_QUESTION`. None has been resolved by assumption.
- No retention period, adequacy decision, breach-notification deadline or consent form requirement has been invented. See the standing rules in [PDPA-2022-ASSUMPTIONS.md](PDPA-2022-ASSUMPTIONS.md) section 2.
